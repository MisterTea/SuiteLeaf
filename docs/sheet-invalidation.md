# Formula invalidation

`cellsToRecalculate` names the formulas that read a changed cell, including a formula that reads one of those formulas. Changing `A1` when `B1` is `=A1` and `C1` is `=B1` returns `B1` and `C1`. `D1`, which is `=E1`, is left out. The cell value is not copied into the result, and the sheet is not changed.

A formula that refers to itself and one other formula is listed once each. A formula whose range is too large to list, such as `SUM(A1:A80)`, is included for any changed cell and marked unlisted, because the cells inside that range are not enumerated. Changing `B1` still includes that formula. `cellsToRecalculate` does not calculate the formulas. `recalculateDirty` copies a direct reference, adds a `SUM` whose range is small enough to list, and rounds one cell. A halfway value rounds away from zero, so `=ROUND(-1.5, 0)` is `-2` and `=ROUND(-15, -1)` is `-20`. `=ROUND(1.5, 0)` stays `2`. `=AVERAGE(A1:B1)` of 10 and 30 becomes 20. A blank cell is left out of that average and out of `COUNT`, `MIN`, and `MAX`. `=COUNT(A1:A2)` is 1 when A2 is blank. `=MIN(A1:B1)` is 10 and `=MAX(A1:B1)` is 30. An average that reads text stays at its previous value. `=TRIM(A1)` removes extra spaces. `  Beds   North  ` becomes `Beds North`. Text is not copied into the address list. A formula error stays at its previous value. `=LEN(A1)` counts characters. `-4` has length 2, and text is counted without being copied into the address list. A formula error stays at its previous value. `=NOT(A1)` is 0 when A1 is a number other than 0, and 1 when A1 is 0 or blank. Text stays at the previous value and is not copied. `=OR(A1, B1)` is 1 when either cell is a number other than 0, and 0 when both are 0. Text stays at the previous value. `=AND(A1, B1)` is 1 when both cells are numbers other than 0, and 0 when either is 0. A blank cell counts as 0. Text stays at the previous value and is not copied into the address list. `=IF(A1, B1, C1)` uses C1 when A1 is 0 and B1 when A1 is any other number. Text in the condition stays at the previous value. The unused cell is not copied. `=ROUND(A1, 2)` of 1.239 becomes 1.24, and a cell that copies that result becomes 1.24. `=ROUND(A1, B1)` uses the number in B1. Text is left at its previous value and is not copied into the address list. When `A1` is `secret tomato`, `B1` is `=A1`, and `C1` is `=B1`, both formulas become that value. `A1` of `10` and `A2` of `5` make `=SUM(A1:A2)` equal `15`. `=SUM(A1:B1)` becomes `20` after `B1` copies `A1`. `=SUM(A1:A3)` stays at its previous value when `A3` is text. `SUM(A1:A80)` is left at its previous value. A cycle of `A1` and `B1` is left unchanged. The address list does not include the cell value, and the sheet passed in is not changed. The formula audit panel lists that same set after the precedents and dependents. For `A1` equal to `=B1` and `B1` equal to `=A1`, the line is `Recalculate: A1, B1.` The panel now names the cells that pass calculated and the cells that were left. A cycle lists `Calculated: none. Left: A1, B1.` A direct reference lists `Calculated: B1.` and a wide sum lists `Left: C1.` The panel does not show the cell value, and it does not write the new value back onto the sheet. The panel was not opened in the browser. The spreadsheet was not opened. This is not Univer recalculation. No speedup is claimed.

`=SUM(Sales[Revenue])` is calculated from that column when the table is passed in. Revenue `10`, `20`, and `5` become `35` on the returned cell. The stored formula stays `=SUM(Sales[Revenue])`. The address list does not include `35` or `secret-value`. Changing `A1` does not include that formula. Changing `C2` does. The audit panel, asked for `C2`, says `Calculated: D1.` and does not show `35` or `secret-value`. The formula bar does not rewrite the formula. The panel was not opened in the browser.
